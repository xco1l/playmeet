# Character coordinate and solver contract

The distributed `kinematic-rig-v2` character is generated by
`scripts/create-character.mjs`. Its primitive envelopes, 104 kg mass allocation,
joint travel and controller parameters are source-informed compatibility
requirements, not independently measured human anatomy. Their provenance and
the replaced asset-pose table are recorded in [the technical audit](PROVENANCE.md).

The generator builds the torso through overlap and clearance variables and
mirrors chains of limb endpoints. Shoulder splay, elbow drop, wrist sweep and
extension ratios define arm directions and reach. Frames follow those directions
and a bending-plane normal. Collider offsets follow the segment construction.
The neutral pose is not read from, or fitted back to, an extracted coordinate
table. A generation method by itself would not establish that distinction.

Distances are metres, masses kilograms, input angles degrees, and angular
velocities radians per second. Positive pitch points downward. The scene uses
positive Y upward and positive Z forward. Generated Euler triples are pitch,
yaw, roll in Babylon's yaw/pitch/roll quaternion convention.

A body's transform origin is its articulation point, not necessarily its centre
of mass. Collider centres are local offsets. Moving every origin to its centre
of mass changes lever arms, reach targets and balance feedback even when the
visible geometry appears identical.

Leg frames have local up pointing downward. Arm frames use local up along the
limb. The elbow intervals carry the left/right distinction while both elbow
limit axes point along positive local Z; mirroring every axis changes the
mechanics.

Havok reports inertia per unit mass in its principal-axis frame. Angular
acceleration must be rotated into that frame, multiplied by inertia, rotated
back, and multiplied by mass and timestep to obtain an angular impulse.

Body creation, constraint insertion and force accumulation preserve the measured
compatibility order. That order is retained from the prototype rather than
presented as a new anatomical design choice. Havok is an iterative solver;
changing it can change a trajectory. The rolling support collides only with the
world, and hands can collide with the trunk. Disabling all self-collision changes
posture and gripping.

The offset hip suspension and anti-stretch restraints are retained mechanical
requirements. Replacing the suspension with a centre-to-centre joint changes
standing height.

The generator reads no research inputs. Its reproducibility check compares the
generated output with the included configuration. Design tests check mirrored
frames, chain-length relationships and the effects of independent design
variables; rejecting Unity field names establishes format separation only.
